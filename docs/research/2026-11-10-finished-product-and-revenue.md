## The finished product people would experience

Fork would be a complete personal cookbook, cooking companion and record of a person's evolving cooking, with useful connections to friends. It should be useful independently of an active social network.

1. **Bring recipes together.** Capture recipes from supported sources, paste or write freely, migrate existing collections, review interpretation and preserve attribution. Groups, quantities, yield and instructions retain their meaning throughout the app.
2. **Find and prepare.** Organize collections, retrieve by useful criteria, choose meals, scale quantities, coordinate shopping and share appropriate household work. Users can begin simply and discover additional capabilities as needed.
3. **Cook confidently.** Read a clear phone/tablet/desktop presentation, keep ingredients accessible, track progress and use contextual timers. Print and explicitly saved offline content provide alternatives. Browser background alarms and cross-device behavior must be validated rather than promised from a checklist.
4. **Remember and improve.** A lightweight Journal connects a cook to its exact recipe version and scale, or to an external source or improvised dish. Private adjustments and next-time notes reappear when useful. Successful variants remain easy to repeat without losing earlier versions.
5. **Share and discover through people.** See what friends actually cooked, save or adapt a recipe, and share selected results with clear audiences. A user need not author a full recipe to participate.
6. **Go deeper when needed.** Later subrecipes, development tools and multi-recipe workflows support demanding cooks. AI, native capabilities and integrations remain evaluated investments, not mandatory ingredients of a successful business.

The differentiating experience is continuity: a recipe travels from capture to planning to cooking to revision without losing meaning, while Fork remembers the person's experience. The product promise can be expressed as: **a dependable home for your recipes that helps your cooking improve over time.**

## How it should look and feel

Keep the annotated-cookbook direction: warm cream surfaces, espresso structure, clay/rust accents, restrained yellow, Bitter editorial headings, Source Sans reading text and intentional mono details. Explore a slightly brassier yellow through complete-screen comparisons; no replacement token is selected here.

The refinement is consistency and restraint: clearer typography roles, coherent page widths and spacing, smaller empty photo areas and oversized heroes, predictable controls, readable comparisons, and thoughtful empty/error/recovery states. Real food photographs should add warmth without requiring every recipe to have a professional image. Progressive disclosure keeps the full roadmap from producing a cluttered interface.

The earlier approximate 8/10 design rating was subjective. **9–9.5/10 is a reasonable ambition for an exceptionally executed finished product**, not a forecast or certification. The goal is the care associated with excellent independent apps and leading consumer products. Matching Apple or NYT Cooking across all dimensions cannot be inferred from a shared aesthetic or completed feature list.

Quality requires observed success with real-device cooking, keyboard/screen-reader use, enlarged text, large and imperfect libraries, imports, interruptions, account boundaries and recovery. An elegant screenshot plus fragile behavior would not meet the ambition.

## What completing the roadmap does for the business

| Investment | Why it matters commercially |
| --- | --- |
| Capture, migration and onboarding | Lowers the cost of trying Fork and moving an existing cooking life into it. |
| Search, collections, fidelity and scaling | Makes Fork credible as a primary cookbook rather than an interesting supplementary tool. |
| Reliable cooking, print and offline | Gives people confidence at the moment of use and removes reasons to return to another app. |
| Journal, history and surfaced adjustments | Creates a distinctive recurring benefit and useful accumulated personal knowledge. |
| Planning, groceries and households | Adds occasions for use between cooking sessions and potential shared paid value. |
| Friends and sharing | Adds trusted inspiration and a potential acquisition route, contingent on real activity. |
| Advanced development tools | Deepens usefulness for a smaller, potentially more committed paying segment. |
| Design consistency and operating reliability | Supports understanding, trust and retention while reducing avoidable support work. |

Together these investments broaden the audience that can choose Fork without sacrificing everyday functionality. That strengthens the business hypothesis substantially. They do not establish demand, justify a numerical conversion uplift or guarantee that the last advanced features outperform investment in acquisition and onboarding. Completing every item before testing would delay the most important evidence.

## Improvements beyond feature completion

### Make previous cooking knowledge immediately useful

Before a repeat cook, show relevant prior changes, scale and next-time notes. After cooking, make recording one useful observation optional and quick. When returning, make repeating the successful version straightforward. Automatically retain reliable facts; do not infer completion or success merely because someone opened cooking mode.

### Design the first week around the person's own recipes

Help someone bring in one existing recipe, cook it, record an adjustment and encounter that adjustment later. Explain capabilities in context instead of requiring a feature tour. Offer bounded personal migration help to early testers to identify friction; convert that learning into self-service improvements rather than an ongoing manual dependency.

### Test a coherent paid offer

Start by testing one clear paid package. Add household pricing when shared value warrants it. Keep enough distinctive value available to experience before purchase and keep basic social participation accessible. Candidate paid benefits include convenient capture, substantial library management, household coordination, deeper development/journal exploration and bounded costly processing. Exact entitlements remain undecided; basic privacy, recovery and existing-content access/export should not become coercive paywalls.

Test US$36 annually rather than assuming US$24 is a ceiling; US$48 average annual revenue per account is an additional sensitivity case, not an endorsed universal price. Higher prices must earn their premium and may reduce conversion or renewal. Existing competitive research already covers Paprika 4 and inexpensive alternatives. A purchased core plus recurring services remains an option; avoid unlimited lifetime obligations for hosted features.

### Build a repeatable route to customers

Test connected cooking groups, a clear migration proposition for existing-app users, and permission-based partnerships with small cooking creators or instructors. Spencer's cooking/café connections may help recruit initial participants, but independent groups are necessary to establish broader demand.

Show specific outcomes in marketing: remembering the changes that worked, preserving an original while making a personal version, or repeating a successful dinner. Make opted-in public recipe links useful to recipients before requiring registration. Follow the roadmap's rights boundaries; do not assume private imports can become public acquisition content.

Treat friend referrals as a hypothesis. Track acquisition through activation and payment, not just signups. Delay substantial advertising until contribution and retained conversion can support it. No outreach, spending or partnerships are authorized by this analysis.

### Keep the operation manageable

Track support burden, correction effort, import failures, processing cost, cancellations and integration maintenance. Build self-service recovery and useful errors. Bound AI costs and avoid a product requiring daily founder-written content to feel alive. Shared models that preserve recipe meaning across views are an operating advantage as well as a quality advantage.

## Revenue potential: arithmetic, not a forecast

All figures below are **USD**. Paying accounts are billing units, not necessarily individual users; household plans complicate that distinction. There is no established timeline or probability for reaching any row, and finishing the roadmap could still produce little revenue if adoption or payment does not materialize.

| Active paying accounts | Gross ARR at $30 average annually | Gross ARR at $36 average annually |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------: | --------------------------------: |
| 100                    |                            $3,000 |                            $3,600 |
| 500                    |                           $15,000 |                           $18,000 |
| 1,000                  |                           $30,000 |                           $36,000 |
| 3,000                  |                           $90,000 |                          $108,000 |
| 5,000                  |                          $150,000 |                          $180,000 |

A few hundred payers could provide meaningful validation and some side revenue. Several thousand could support a solo business if acquisition, renewal and costs cooperate. Five thousand would be a substantial achievement, not an expected result of implementation.

Using the existing model's illustrative 5.6% fee allowance and $8.40 annual variable reserve per paid account gives annual contribution before fixed costs of `0.944 × annual price − 8.40`:

| Average annual price | Contribution per payer | Payers for $60,000 founder compensation plus $4,200 fixed costs |
| --- | --: | --: |
| $24 | $14.26 | 4,504 |
| $30 | $19.92 | 3,223 |
| $36 | $25.58 | 2,510 |
| $48 | $36.91 | 1,740 |

The count uses unrounded contribution. These thresholds exclude acquisition spending, taxes, refunds/disputes, professional advice, native-store commissions and founder development time. Free-user costs must fit the budgets or be added. Household usage and advanced features may raise costs. This is a sensitivity model, not take-home pay or accounting profit. A lower customer count at a higher price does not mean those customers are equally easy to acquire.

The authoritative roadmap's existing month-24 scenarios remain unchanged: base approximately $8,262 ARR and upside approximately $57,024 ARR, with annualized modeled contribution after operating costs of approximately $4,046 and $33,664 respectively. Those scenarios are assumption-driven, not measured expectations; the mature customer-count table above is not a replacement month-24 forecast.

Retention also determines required acquisition. With 3,000 paying accounts, annual renewal of 70%, 80% or 90% requires 900, 600 or 300 replacement payers just to remain level. At an illustrative 5% registration-to-paid rate, replacing 600 requires 12,000 new registrations annually. These are steady-state illustrations, not benchmarks; conversion delay and cohort timing matter in a real model.

## What to expect and what to validate

The finished vision is a serious, polished everyday cooking product with a distinctive memory and social layer. It is credible to aim for excellent independent-app quality and eventual solo-business revenue. Neither is automatic: quality depends on execution in real conditions; revenue additionally depends on distribution, paid value and retention.

Proceed through evidence milestones while continuing the roadmap: useful without founder reminders; useful to independent groups; first actual paid customers; a repeatable acquisition route; sustained renewal and manageable costs. Observe return cooking, prior notes used, voluntary invitations and household participation. Judge optional reminders by whether they help an expressed cooking intention, not by notification clicks.

No new broad feature hunt is proposed. Beyond the existing roadmap, the highest-value work is an excellent first week, useful recall at the next cook, a clear paid offer, repeatable acquisition and an operation one person can maintain.
