# Guide Fork with cookbook design research

**Design direction for serious home cooks · 8 November 2026**

**Keep Fork’s warm cookbook identity and improve how its hierarchy changes with the cook’s task.** “A beautifully edited cookbook with the precision of a great productivity app” is a sound direction: photography and typography make recipes inviting; predictable controls, faithful quantities and recoverable work make them dependable. The strongest reference mix extends beyond Todoist, Obsidian and Claude to Bear, Things, Linear, Mela, Paprika and Crouton. Borrow their specific mechanisms, not their combined feature lists or visual signatures. Research supports clear state, contextual information and access to the whole task; it does not establish an ideal beige palette, serif font or kitchen layout. The immediate opportunity is to refine Cook entry and action emphasis, protect authoring, and test complete screens on real devices. Cookbook, Journal and Community remain the approved product pillars, with the friends pilot proceeding alongside cookbook completion.

## Preserve the identity and sharpen its purpose

The existing cream, espresso, clay and marigold palette, Bitter headings and Source Sans reading text already form a coherent editorial language. The earlier desktop assessment found useful photography, calm browse cards, plain-language version notes and strong ingredients/method columns. Its most consequential issues concerned task hierarchy: Cook retained a roughly 426px introductory hero at a 1280×720 viewport; Fork had stronger emphasis than Cook; and a history link remained after its target disappeared in Cook. Those are bounded observations from 8 November, not findings from a mobile or authenticated-editor test. The live homepage also differed from already-improved local content, so check release parity before duplicating that work. ([Earlier UI assessment](/Users/spfle/Dev/Fork/.impeccable/critique/2026-11-08T05-48-56Z__src-routes-recipes-slug-page-svelte.md))

Serious home cooks remain the primary audience. Chef-adjacent precision belongs in clear yields, quantities, reusable components and revision records; it does not require restaurant operations or a denser default interface.

Three design languages should work together. **Editorial cookbook** means an identifiable dish, thoughtful type, meaningful grouping and generous separation between sections. **Restrained utility** means consistent action placement, clear labels, inspectable interpretation and visible persistence state. **Cooking workspace** means readable quantities, quick access to ingredients and method, freely accessible overview and reliable return after interruption. The first supplies character; the other two determine whether that character survives everyday use. This combination is a design judgment for Fork, not a proven formula or an instruction to make every screen equally sparse.

The visual proposal is deliberately conservative. Keep paper `#f7f3ec` and espresso/ink as the reading foundation; let food photography supply the largest areas of varied color. Retain clay `#b23c00` for established editorial and interactive roles, making links identifiable beyond color. Retain marigold `#eca620` for the strongest action emphasis where its dark label remains legible. **Change which action receives emphasis before changing the entire accent system.** Selection, warnings and successful saving should not all share an unexplained yellow signal. Labels, icons or shape must carry meaning too. Apple recommends consistent color semantics and warns that nearby artwork affects color perception; it does not prescribe Fork’s palette. ([Apple color guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color))

Photography should dominate the invitation, not the working instructions. Browse and recipe overview benefit from showing what the dish is; a cook who deliberately enters Cook has already chosen it. Start by bringing the working recipe into view and preserving place. Trial a compact identity/header only if that transition remains insufficient. Keep a path back to the photograph and recipe context. Attractive imagery is a taste and recognition choice here, not evidence of fewer cooking mistakes. A page must also work with a pale dish, an imperfect phone photo or no photograph; missing imagery should not force a decorative substitute that implies an actual cooked result.

Retain the current type families while testing functional hierarchy. Titles can carry cookbook character; quantities, ingredient names, preparation qualifiers and action labels need immediate legibility. Check fractions, ranges, `1` versus `l`, degree signs, long names and wrapped instructions. Increase section separation before adding more bordered boxes; use alignment and weight before muting text into faintness. Preserve the considered desktop method width and mobile Ingredients/Method switch until task evidence justifies changing them. Font research found substantial individual differences and that preference did not predict reading speed; it cannot select Fork’s universal winning font. ([Wallace et al., reading research](https://shaunwallace.org/files/Readability__TOCHI.pdf))

Exact type sizes, spacing, line measure, accent proportions and hero dimensions remain hypotheses. Compare a complete current screen with one quieter refinement using identical recipes, images and capabilities. Judge whether cooks notice the right thing and complete the task, as well as whether the screen feels beautifully edited. Restraint means lowering competition around important information, not hiding every control.

## Borrow mechanisms from a broad reference set

The useful comparison is between decisions, not entire brands. The product sources below document behavior or explain vendor intent; they do not establish that one product is more usable, faster or more beautiful than another. The reference research reviewed documentation rather than authenticated workflows, and its attempted screenshot retrievals did not provide usable visual inspection. These are therefore defensible interaction references and a shortlist for later visual review, not a ranked aesthetic benchmark.

The broader lesson is consistency around a durable content object. Bear and Obsidian suggest an editor that resembles its output; Things suggests optional focus with recoverable navigation; Linear suggests stable action placement; cooking products supply domain-specific navigation and quantity patterns. Todoist and Claude remain useful examples, not governing authorities. Competitor convergence is positive evidence that a job deserves consideration, but neither popularity nor feature prevalence settles the best implementation for Fork.

## Let evidence constrain taste without pretending to decide it

**Four evidence levels matter.** Standards establish requirements; empirical studies establish bounded observations; product documentation and design guidance provide mechanisms and rationales; Fork-specific judgments select what to prototype. A visual preference cannot become a usability result merely by citing a guideline nearby.

Beauty and usability deserve separate measures. A 2000 simulated-ATM experiment found relationships between perceived aesthetics and perceived usability. A 2012 online-shopping experiment found no aesthetic effect on perceived usability under its conditions. Neither establishes that an attractive recipe interface prevents errors. The useful conclusion is to ask about visual appeal separately from observed completion, recovery and understanding. ([Tractinsky, Katz and Ikar](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/what-is-beautiful-is-usable-2/), [Tuch et al.](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.wwu.edu/dist/8/2868/files/2018/04/Tuch-et-al-2012-Is-Beautiful-Usable-2don1em.pdf))

Cooking research supports access to context, with important limits. Buykx’s thesis reports two studies of 24 university students each: integrated ingredient information improved perceived ease in one comparison without significantly changing total reading time; pictures were liked without reducing overall problems in the second study. A 2024 contextual inquiry with eight participants cooking one dish in their own kitchens observed looking ahead to coordinate parallel work and check progress. Together they justify testing contextual quantities and free navigation, not declaring a next-only wizard, inline quantities or photographs universally superior. ([Buykx thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/5158/1/Lucy%20Buykx%20accepted%20thesis.pdf), [Barquero et al., cooking task guidance](https://www.ruizlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/3643834.3661611.pdf))

Interruption and access deserve equal attention. Interviews with 20 blind experienced cooks and four instructors describe dirty-hand interaction, losing place and repeated irrelevant information; voice introduced its own navigation and state problems. A separate 12-undergraduate interruption prototype study had inconclusive performance findings. The responsible inference is to preserve place, reduce unnecessary interaction and support multiple accessible routes—not to promise that voice or automatic tracking solves kitchen attention. ([Non-visual cooking instructions study](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.19065v1), [Cook’s Collage study](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/fce/ahri/publications/dj.pdf))

**WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, not the definition of comfortable cooking.** Normal text generally needs 4.5:1 contrast; qualifying large text needs 3:1. The AA pointer-target criterion generally requires 24×24 CSS pixels or qualifying exceptions. Also test keyboard operation, visible and unobscured focus, non-drag alternatives, accessible names/status messages, 200% text resizing and narrow reflow. A selected checklist does not establish full conformance. The earlier audit’s calculated 4.46:1 secondary-text/hero pairing needs verification and correction if used for normal text; thresholds cannot be rounded upward. ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [contrast interpretation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [target-size interpretation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html))

Prototype roughly 44 CSS-pixel main cooking hit areas where practical, with clear separation from destructive actions. This is a product preference beyond the AA minimum, not a proven kitchen optimum or a conversion of native points into CSS pixels. Test counter-distance reading, enlarged text and actual screen-reader use. Motion should explain state or location without delaying repeated input; reduced-motion preferences should remove unnecessary spatial movement while retaining feedback. ([WebKit motion guidance](https://webkit.org/blog/7551/responsive-design-for-motion/))

## Give each screen a clear working contract

**Browse and search:** make the dish and its name primary, then the few facts needed to choose it. Keep the existing calm photo/title presentation and useful visible filters. Distinguish a personal cookbook from public discovery and friends’ activity; a search scope must remain understandable after filtering. Show why a result matched without overwhelming the title, preserve query and filter state on return, and provide an actionable empty state. Test known-item retrieval with an ordinary personal library, not only a three-recipe seed. These extend the roadmap’s existing search and organization decisions. ([Product roadmap](</Users/spfle/Dev/Fork/reports/Fork product roadmap.md>))

**Recipe overview:** combine photographic invitation with practical orientation: yield/servings, relevant time, source and the action the viewer came to take. Give Cook clear emphasis for an available recipe; saved state and ownership can change the prominence of other actions. Explain “Save to cookbook” versus “Make my version” without Git knowledge. Keep history available through meaningful summaries and progressive disclosure, not equal-weight tabs competing with the recipe. Every visible link must work in its current state, including Cook. A cook should understand what changed without opening a technical diff, while detailed comparison remains available.

**Editor and import:** preserve the existing paste-friendly editor, retained unparsed lines, one-save model, failed-save text recovery and concurrency protection. These were found in source; their presence is not a completed runtime validation. Make common fields easy to find and optional detail progressively available. Keep quantities and preparation qualifiers visibly connected. Import should enter the same correction flow with source material reachable, uncertain interpretation exposed and publication deliberate. Paprika’s scan documentation explicitly supports comparison and correction before saving; it is a useful precedent for review, not a reason to add OCR to this pass. ([Existing capability audit](</Users/spfle/Dev/Fork/research_notes/Fork product roadmap/current_product_audit.md>), [Paprika scan correction](https://www.paprikaapp.com/help/v4/ios/scanningrecipes/))

**Accepted editor refinement — 9 November 2026:** an optional collapsible **Insert** toolbar adds supported plain-text elements, initially section headings. Inserting a heading and typing `# Dough` produce the same result: a larger, heavier heading line with the `#` still visible in a softer readable color. Preserve markers when copying/pasting, and retain clear interpretation feedback. Ordinary recipes need no syntax; yield and visibility remain normal fields, and Cooklang stays an optional advanced path. Validate a small live-formatting prototype for cursor/selection, undo, paste, mobile input and keyboard/screen-reader access before extending the editor. This is an accepted product direction, not an empirical finding that this interaction outperforms every alternative.

Save feedback must name the actual promise: unsaved changes, saving, saved version or failed save. Under the 8 November MVP-first decision, durable recovery after closing/reloading is a post-MVP nice-to-have, not P0 work or a launch gate. Do not label unsaved input as a recoverable local draft before that capability exists. Preserve text through failure and conflict, state any photo-recovery limit, and never imply cross-device protection from a local draft. Row removal/reordering needs a comprehensible recovery path and sensible focus. Field-level undo, draft recovery and restoring history are different operations. MVP recipe deletion is owner-only permanent deletion with clear confirmation; Trash and deleted-recipe restore are post-MVP. Preserve independent forks and handle references to a deleted original clearly. Nielsen’s visibility, recognition and recovery heuristics support this direction; they do not mandate a particular toolbar. ([Usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/))

**Cook:** expose the working recipe immediately, retain an obvious exit, preserve the current desktop columns and keep mobile ingredient access from losing method position. Distinguish “I am viewing this step” from “I completed this step”; looking ahead must not mark previous work done. Fork already has independently tickable ingredients/steps and version-scoped persistence, so inspect and refine those semantics rather than proposing progress tracking as absent. Test resuming after switching recipes, backgrounding and a short interruption, plus deliberately starting a fresh cook. Do not silently rewrite author instructions into shortened steps.

Make active scale visible and distinguish temporary serving changes from a permanent revision. Contextual ingredient quantities should reference authoritative data, with divided ingredients handled explicitly; arbitrary imported prose is not automatically linked. Preserve “to taste,” ranges and unparsed wording. Timings, temperatures and pan sizes must not be multiplied as ordinary ingredient quantities. Future concurrent timers need names and persistent context. Browser wake locks and background timers have platform limitations, so foreground success must never become a promise of locked-screen alarms. ([Wake-lock constraints](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Screen_Wake_Lock_API), [browser timer constraints](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/setTimeout))

**Later graphical cooking view:** Spencer’s accepted direction adds an optional power-user view of ingredients flowing into steps, component dependencies, parallel branches and their joins. Keep it alongside the standard instructions, with shared version, scale and progress. Prototype in P4 with Cooklang-backed recipes as the proposed initial scope: start with preserved ingredient-to-step references, and show prerequisite/parallel branches only where relationships are authored or reviewed. Cooklang structure alone does not establish those dependencies. Duration data is needed for a timed layout, not for the basic graph. Extend across pinned subrecipes as they become available. This is distinct from recipe ancestry and remains a substantial later build. The cooking research motivates testing coordination and overview, but does not establish that a graph will outperform the current layout. Test whether cooks can trace ingredients, identify prerequisites and coordinate parallel work without excessive authoring effort. ([Roadmap scope](</Users/spfle/Dev/Fork/reports/Fork product roadmap.md>))

**Journal and social:** make recording dinner lighter than writing a recipe. Start with dish/date, optional own photo or note, and an exact recipe/version/scale or external source when available. An improvised meal is valid. Distinguish “Cooked this,” “Note for next time” and an intentional new recipe version. Show audience before sharing and keep private notes separate. A friend’s source-linked event should lead to permitted source/save/cook actions without exposing a private imported recipe. Photography can tell the outcome; conversation and counts should support cooking rather than dominate it. This follows the approved early social direction, not a new requirement to postpone Community until the cookbook is complete. ([Social pilot decisions](</Users/spfle/Dev/Fork/research_notes/Fork social launch strategy/social_launch.md>))

A draft design contract can now guide implementation: **food leads discovery; the working recipe leads Cook; controls stay predictable; emphasis has consistent meaning; quantities and source survive every view; saved state is truthful; recovery limits are clear; audience is explicit.** Preserve desktop columns, intentional mobile switching and progressively disclosed history as the baseline. Any proposal to change them should name the observed problem, compare alternatives with equal content, and show the improvement. This contract refines the current identity without rewriting DESIGN.md or superseding product decisions.

Use two complementary learning tracks. For formative interface work, pilot tasks with two cooking friends, then recruit roughly four to six nonfriends with relevant habits and varied devices; include accessibility needs directly. These are practical sample suggestions, not statistically justified thresholds. Observe finding a dinner recipe, locating a quantity, correcting ingredients, recognizing save state, recovering an accidental action and resuming after an interruption. Test realistic fractions, divided ingredients, multiple components, long instructions and missing photos. Compare one restrained alternative with the current design, varying presentation while holding capability constant and counterbalancing order where practical.

Record unassisted completion, wrong actions/quantities, help, lost place, recovery and participants’ explanation of save/audience state. Ask about aesthetics after the task and keep preference separate from performance. Report “three of these six participants missed Save,” not a population percentage. Then use the existing friends pilot to observe ordinary repeat cooking and the source→save→cook loop over time, distinguishing founder prompts from independent use. Independent participants check clarity; independent friend clusters later check whether social usefulness survives outside Spencer’s circle. Neither small track proves retention, market demand or universal design superiority.

Fork’s next design gain should be visible in a cook’s behavior: choosing a dish without decoding technical language, entering the work without scrolling past its introduction, correcting a recipe without fearing lost text, and remembering a useful change after dinner. Keep aesthetic ambition high, but make these moments the test of precision. No new real-device study, authenticated workflow validation or rendered competitor comparison underpins this report; those are the next evidence-producing steps, not reasons for a wholesale redesign.
